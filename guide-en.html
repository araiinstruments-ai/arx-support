1. What is AR-X?

AR-X is an app for making music on your iPhone or iPad. You don't need a piano or a guitar. Just touch the screen to play drums and melodies.

Think of it as building blocks made of sound. You line up a drum block, a melody block and a synth block (an instrument that makes strange and wonderful sounds) to build a song that is all your own.

What you can do:

  • Play drums (boom, tap, tss-tss)
  • Line up notes and repeat them (a sequencer)
  • Change the synth sound (soft sounds, sharp sounds, and more)
  • Make fun sounds with effects (draw on the XY pad with your finger)
  • Save your songs and recall them later
  • Record your own performance
  • Connect a MIDI keyboard or other music apps

2. Make your first sound

Just three things to do, and you'll hear music.

  1. Open the app. Tap AR-X on your Home Screen. Turn off your iPhone's silent switch and turn the volume up.
  2. Tap "PLAY". The button lights up and the rhythm starts.
  3. Tap "PLAY" again. The light goes out and the sound stops.

To change the tempo (speed), turn the round knob labeled BPM with your finger. Turn it right to go faster, left to go slower.

No sound? See "14. Troubleshooting".

A knob is a round dial you turn with your finger. A fader is a bar you slide up and down. Buttons are tapped.

There are two ways to change pages.

  • Use <PAGE and PAGE> to move to the neighbor. They cycle through pages 1→2→3→4→5→6 (MIDI and REC can't be reached with these buttons).

Only the button for the page you're on is lit.

5. Play the drums

Make the rhythm on "DRUM CTRL" (page 5) and change the sounds on "DRUM EDIT" (page 6).

Make a rhythm (DRUM CTRL)

  1. Tap the "DRUM CTRL" button.
  2. Buttons are lined up in four rows: KICK (boom), SNR (tap), CHH (closed hi-hat, "tss") and OHH (open hi-hat, "tsss").
  3. Each row has buttons "1–16". Tap a button and a sound plays at that spot (it lights up). Tap again to turn it off.
  4. Press PLAY and the sounds play from 1 to 16 in order, then loop back around.

For example, put KICK on "1, 5, 9, 13" and you get the classic "boom, boom, boom, boom" beat.

Under each step there is also a small "ACC" button. It means "accent". Light it up and that sound plays a little stronger.

Patterns A / B / C

You can keep three separate rhythms. Tap PTN A / PTN B / PTN C on the left to switch. For example, use A for a "calm rhythm" and B for a "busy rhythm".

Change the sounds (DRUM EDIT)

Tap "DRUM EDIT" and knobs appear for each drum. KICK, SNARE and HI HAT have these knobs.

KnobWhen to turn it
TUNEPitch of the sound. Right = higher, left = lower
DECAYHow long the sound rings. HI HAT also has O.DECAY for the open sound
LEVELVolume of that drum
DISTSwitch that distorts the drum (rough and crunchy)
CRSHSwitch that makes the drum gritty (the crusher)
FX1–FX4How much to send to each effect

At the bottom right of the page are the shared DISTORTION and CRUSH settings.

  • DISTORTION: DRIVE (how strong), LEVEL (volume), HARD / MID / SOFT (type of distortion)
  • CRUSH: FREQ, LEVEL, 12BIT / 8BIT / 4BIT (the smaller the number, the grittier)

6. Make a melody

You can play two melodies at once: "SEQ A" and "SEQ B". Think of a guitar and a bass. Turn each on or off with the "SEQ A" and "SEQ B" buttons on the left.

Set the pitch (SEQ CTRL)

  1. Tap the "SEQ CTRL" button (page 1).
  2. Slide PITCH (the vertical bar) up and down. Up is a higher sound, down is a lower sound.
  3. The + / − buttons move the sound one more octave (from one "do" to the next "do") higher or lower.
  4. Turn the 4BIT PTN knob to change the shape of "when sounds play". Turning right makes patterns with more notes.
  5. The small lights (LEDs) above the knob show where the sound plays now.

In short: PITCH is "which note", and 4BIT PTN is "when to play it".

Set strength and length (SEQ EDIT)

On the "SEQ EDIT" button (page 2) you can fine-tune more.

ThisWhat it does
V1 / V2 buttonsChoose how strongly the note plays (velocity)
V0 / V1 / V2 knobsSet the strength amounts themselves
GATE (vertical bar)Note length. Up = longer, down = short and snappy
TRANSPOSE (knob)Every 4 bars, shift the whole melody's pitch at once

With TRANSPOSE, the same melody can change over time, for example "normal in bar 1, a little lower in bar 3".

Tip: set SEQ A and SEQ B to slightly different pitches for pretty harmonies (notes layered together).

7. Change the synth sound

A synth is an instrument that makes strange sounds. AR-X uses a method called FM to make sparkly, bell-like and metallic sounds.

What is FM?

When a "main sound" is wobbled by a "wobbler sound", you get a bubbly, mysterious tone. It's like pushing a swing while another person shakes its chains: the motion gets more complicated.

  • CAR (carrier): the main sound
  • MOD (modulator): the wobbler sound

Page 3: SYN CTRL

  • ON (buttons): for each of the 8 steps, whether to use that spot's settings.
  • GRIDE: notes connect with a smooth slide, like a snake gliding.
  • GRIDE TIME / MGR TIME (knobs): how long the slide takes.
  • STEP SEQ / LOOP X2 / AS 8TH: switches that choose the shape of the repeat.

Page 4: SYN EDIT

This is the "sound workshop". There are many knobs, so at first it's plenty of fun to touch only these (we recommend TUNE, MOD IDX, ATK and REL).

Knob / buttonRole
TUNEShifts the pitch of everything
GAINVolume
MOD IDXHow big the wobbler is (higher = sparkly and gritty)
MOD FBKFeeds the wobbler back into itself for a thick, gooey sound
MOD RATE / CAR RATEPitch ratio of the wobbler and the main sound
SIN / TRI / SQR / SAWSound shape. SIN is soft, SQR and SAW are sharp
ATK / DEC / SUS / RELShape of the start and end of a sound (see below)
HI PASS / LO PASSCut high sounds or low sounds
SERIES / PARALLELApply the wobbler to the main sound, or play the two side by side
LFO RT / DEP / DELA vibrato-like wobble. Speed, depth, and delay before it starts
UNISON ONLayers the same sound slightly shifted to make it thick

FX1–FX4Send a little to the effects

What are ATK / DEC / SUS / REL? A piano goes "pong!" and quickly gets quieter; a violin swells gently and fades slowly. These four settings decide that "shape".

  • ATK (attack): time it takes to reach full volume
  • DEC (decay): time to fall from full volume to a comfortable level
  • SUS (sustain): volume while you hold the note
  • REL (release): time to fade out after you let go

Nothing can break, so turn things and hunt for fun sounds.

8. Play with effects (XY pad)

Effects are “magic” you add to the sound. You can make it muffled, or add an echo like a mountain call. The square pad on the left edge is the XY PAD.

How to use it

  1. Use the FX EDIT button to choose the effect to play with (FX1–FX4).
  2. Touch the pad with your finger. The sound changes with your horizontal (X) and vertical (Y) position.
  3. Use the FX MIX knob to choose how much of the effect you hear.
  4. Light up FX HOLD and the effect stays at that position even after you lift your finger.

The four effects

EffectWhat it sounds likeHorizontal (X)Vertical (Y)
FX1Muffled sound (low-pass filter)Right = bright, left = muffledUp = stronger character
FX2Whooshing like a jet plane (flanger)Speed of the sweepStronger effect
FX3Mountain-call echo (delay)Gap between echoesHow long the echoes keep repeating
FX4Bath or concert-hall ring (reverb)Length of the ringSoftness of the sound

Which instrument gets the effect?

Use the FX1–FX4 buttons on the drum and synth pages (DRUM EDIT, SYN EDIT) to choose which instrument you want. The instrument's sound is sent to each lit effect.

9. BPM and balance

BPM (speed)

BPM is "how many beats per minute". The bigger the number, the faster the song.

  • Turn the BPM knob on the left.
  • The range is 40 to 240. It starts at 120.
  • Check the current number in the display just above.

In a game, think of it as deciding a character's "walking speed".

BALANCE SYN-DRM

This knob balances the loudness of the synth (SYN) and the drums (DRM). Like a seesaw, making one louder makes the other quieter.

  • Middle: synth and drums are nicely balanced
  • Turn left: drums sound louder
  • Turn right: synth sounds louder

This knob is on every page, so you can use it anytime.

10. Saving and recalling

You can tuck away your sounds and rhythms like putting them in a trunk. Learn a few rules and it isn't hard.

The keys, simply

  • USER A / USER B / USER C: three "boxes". These buttons choose the box.
  • Slots 1, 2, 3, 4: the room numbers inside a box. Each box holds 4.
  • SAVE: the button you press to "store what's on this page".

Note that saves are kept separately for each page. For example, something saved on DRUM CTRL can only be recalled on DRUM CTRL.

Save

  1. Open the page you want to save.
  2. Choose USER A, B or C.
  3. Tap SAVE. The button lights up.
  4. Tap the number (1–4) you want to save to.
  5. When asked to confirm, tap YES. NO means "don't store it here", and CANCEL means "never mind".

On SEQ CTRL / SEQ EDIT, it asks once more, "Save SEQ A?", so you don't overwrite something by accident.

[Important] If you save to a number that already has something in it, the old content is erased. Use an empty number for sounds you care about.

Recall

  1. Open the page you want to recall on.
  2. Choose USER A / B / C.
  3. Without pressing SAVE, tap the number (1–4).

If it says "empty", nothing has been saved in that number yet.

The app comes with four demo melodies and synth sounds (USER A). Feel free to overwrite them.

Turn a project into a file (EXPORT / IMPORT)

The "REC EXPORT" page has EXPORT and IMPORT buttons.

  • EXPORT: turns your current work into a file that you can save or send from the share menu.
  • IMPORT: pick a saved file to bring your work back.

Handy when you reinstall the app or want to give a friend your work.

11. Sync tempo with other apps (Ableton Link)

Link is a way for everyone to "play at the same tempo". It's like a band all watching the drummer to keep the rhythm together. You can match tempo and start with other apps that support the same technology, or with other iPhones and iPads.

How to use it

  1. Use the app together with other devices on the same Wi‑Fi.
  2. Tap the "LINK" button on the left.
  3. The Link settings open. Turn the switch on.
  4. The first time, if iPhone asks whether to allow "Local Network" access, choose "Allow".
  5. While it looks for friends, it shows "Ableton Link: searching for peers…". Once found, the tempo matches automatically.

To turn it off, tap "LINK" again.

Don't worry

  • Even with no friends around, the app works normally.
  • If you turn Link off, the sound that's playing keeps going.
  • While Link is on, pressing PLAY starts aligned with the beat position of the other apps.

12. Connect a MIDI keyboard or controller

MIDI is like a "language" that connects instruments and controllers to apps. Connect a device with knobs and faders and you can move the on-screen knobs with real ones.

Connect and link (LEARN)

  1. Connect your controller to the iPhone or iPad.
  2. Open the MIDI page with the "MIDI" button.
  3. Choose your device from the list on the "MIDI" page. If it says "Connected", you're good.
  4. Tap LEARN (the button lights up).
  5. Tap a red button, knob or fader on the screen.
  6. Move the real control on your controller. When it says "learned", the link is done.

Repeat to link as many as you like. The links are remembered even after you close the app.

Easier linking (NAV LEARN)

Press NAV LEARN and the app tells you in order, "move this one", "now this one". Just move your controller's knobs in turn to link lots of controls. To stop, press NAV LEARN again.

Start over

Press CLEAR ALL to erase every link.

Don't be surprised

Sometimes the sound doesn't change right away when you move a knob on your controller. This is a safety feature so the on-screen knob doesn't suddenly jump. Move your knob to where the on-screen knob is, and they connect and start moving together.

"MIDI MONITOR" shows the signals arriving now, which is handy to check that everything is connected.

13. Recording (REC)

You can save the sound you made as an audio file (WAV), like recording to a cassette tape. It records the sound playing in the app directly, so you don't need a microphone.

How to do it

  1. Open the recording page with the "REC EXPORT" button.
  2. Choose how long to record: MANUAL (you stop it), 1 BAR, 2 BARS, 4 BARS, 8 BARS or 16 BARS.
  3. Press PLAY to start the sound.
  4. Tap REC. It shows "● REC…" and the seconds so far.
  5. With MANUAL, tap REC again to stop. If you chose a number of bars, it stops automatically at that length.
  6. When it stops, a WAV file is made.

"1 bar" means "4 beats". At BPM 120, one bar is 2 seconds.

Set the tempo and balance before you record, and you'll have fewer retakes.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
No soundTurn up your iPhone volume. Turn off the side switch (silent mode). Check that you pressed PLAY. Check that KICK, SNARE, HAT, SEQ A and SEQ B are lit (unlit means silent).
Only the drums, or only the synth, is quietCheck whether the BALANCE SYN-DRM knob is too far to one side, and try returning it to the middle.
Sound stops when I go to the Home ScreenIn iPhone "Settings" → AR-X, check that "Background Playback" is on. If on, it keeps playing on the Home Screen. If off, it stops when you leave the app (this saves battery).
Sound skips or rhythm driftsClose other apps. Check that the device isn't hot. If that doesn't help, close the app once and open it again.
Only the synth sound disappearedClose the app once and open it again. If it still isn't fixed, please tell us when and on which page it happened.
Link can't find friendsCheck that you're on the same Wi‑Fi. In iPhone "Settings", check that AR-X's "Local Network" is on. Turn LINK off, then on again.
MIDI device not foundReconnect the cable. Choose the device again on the MIDI page.
I accidentally erased my soundWhen saving, answer the "Save?" question with YES carefully. Keep important work safe by turning it into a file with EXPORT.
